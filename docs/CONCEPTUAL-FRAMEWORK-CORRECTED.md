# Conceptual Framework (Corrected)

The conceptual framework for TechConnect illustrates the theoretical foundation and relationships between key components that guide the development of a peer tutoring platform for the College of Industrial Technology. At its core, the framework recognizes three primary stakeholders: administrators who oversee system operations, tutors who provide academic support, and learners who seek assistance. These stakeholders interact through a technology-mediated environment that facilitates peer learning through structured processes. The framework positions technology as an enabler rather than a replacement for human interaction, ensuring that digital tools enhance rather than diminish the personal connections that make peer learning effective.

The framework is grounded in Vygotsky's Zone of Proximal Development theory, which emphasizes that students learn most effectively when guided by more knowledgeable peers. This theoretical foundation drives the platform's approach to connecting students with tutors who possess the specific expertise needed. The framework also incorporates principles of self-directed learning, recognizing that while technology can empower student autonomy, structured mentorship remains crucial for effective learning outcomes. These theoretical underpinnings inform design decisions throughout the platform, from the learner-driven tutor selection process to the peer-to-peer video communication and collaborative tools that support synchronous learning interactions.

Central to this framework is the concept of transparent filtering and learner-driven selection, which allows students to browse available tutors and make informed choices based on subject expertise, availability, ratings, and feedback. This filtering process considers multiple factors including tutor qualifications, schedule compatibility, and performance ratings to help learners find suitable tutoring partners. The framework emphasizes learner autonomy and transparency in the selection process, ensuring students maintain control over their learning partnerships rather than relying on opaque algorithmic assignments that may not reflect individual preferences or learning styles.

The conceptual framework further recognizes the importance of incentive structures in sustaining peer tutoring programs. By incorporating recognition mechanisms such as certificates, voluntary QR code-based donations, and portfolio-building opportunities, the platform addresses the challenge of maintaining consistent tutor participation. This aligns with motivational theories that emphasize both intrinsic rewards (knowledge reinforcement, leadership development) and extrinsic benefits (voluntary financial appreciation, resume enhancement). The integration of peer-to-peer real-time collaboration tools including WebRTC-based video communication, interactive whiteboards using Canvas API, and real-time chat functionality supports rich learning interactions that replicate and extend the affordances of face-to-face tutoring without requiring centralized video infrastructure.

The framework acknowledges the critical role of feedback and quality assurance mechanisms in maintaining high tutoring standards. Structured feedback systems using eleven predefined tags (Clear Explanations, Patient & Friendly, Well Prepared, Helpful Materials, Great Communication, Always On Time, Very Knowledgeable, Helped Me Improve, Engaging Session, Good Examples, Responsive) enable systematic evaluation of tutor performance, providing actionable insights for improvement while helping learners make informed selection decisions. Rating aggregation and display provide transparency about tutor quality, though the framework recognizes the importance of balancing quality indicators against fairness for new tutors who lack established track records. Administrative oversight and resource management features ensure platform sustainability while maintaining appropriate quality standards.
